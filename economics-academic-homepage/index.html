Postdoctoral Fellow in Economics
HKU Business School, The University of Hong Kong

I am a postdoctoral fellow in economics at HKU Business School. My research lies at the intersection of open-economy macroeconomics, macro-finance, and industrial organization.

I study how financial frictions, asset markets, and firm-level incentives shape capital flows, aggregate fluctuations, and long-run growth. My work combines dynamic structural modelling, quantitative estimation, and policy analysis.

Working Papers

Asset Bubbles, International Capital Flow and Business Cycle

Ph.D. Dissertation

Develops an asymmetric two-country DSGE model in which rational asset bubbles work as sentiment shocks. The project studies why capital may flow from high-growth emerging economies to developed economies, and quantifies the mechanism using China-US quarterly data.

Paper available upon request

Housing Market Liquidity, Labor Misallocation, and TFP

Working Paper

Builds a multi-sector endogenous-growth framework to study housing as both financial collateral and a production factor. It examines how credit expansion changes labor allocation, innovation incentives, and productivity.
